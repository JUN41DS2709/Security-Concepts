# Attack Surface in Cybersecurity

An **attack surface** in cybersecurity is all the possible entry points where an unauthorized user (attacker) can gain access, exfiltrate data, run malicious code, steal sensitive information, or disrupt services.

## Example

Imagine a house, and you are a thief planning to rob it.

Before breaking in, what is the first thing you would do?

Of course, you would not go straight and knock on the front door. First, you would monitor the house, gather information (**reconnaissance / recon**), and then look for something you can take advantage of to enter the house.

That entry point could be:
- Windows
- Chimneys
- AC vents
- Back doors

Similarly, in cybersecurity, these possible entry points are called the **attack surface** because they can help an attacker gain unauthorized access to a system.

---

# Types of Attack Surface

## 1. Digital Attack Surface

The digital attack surface includes all internet-facing or digital technologies, software, applications, and services that an attacker may target.

### Examples
- Websites
- Cloud storage
- Email services
- Databases
- APIs

**Example:**

An organization has a website, a cloud environment, and a database. All of these are part of its **digital attack surface**.

---

## 2. Physical Attack Surface

The physical attack surface includes physical devices and infrastructure that an attacker can directly access.

### Examples
- Laptops
- Mobile phones
- USB drives
- Servers
- Networking devices

**Example:**

An attacker enters an organization and plugs a USB drive containing malware into an employee's laptop, compromising the system.

---

## 3. Human (Social Engineering) Attack Surface

Humans can also become part of the attack surface because attackers can manipulate them using **social engineering**.

**Example:**

An employee unknowingly clicks on an email that appears to be from his daughter, but it is actually a phishing email sent by an attacker. As a result, the employee's system gets compromised.

---

## Key Point

An attack surface is **every possible way an attacker can interact with a target to gain unauthorized access or cause damage**. The larger the attack surface, the more opportunities an attacker has to exploit the target.
